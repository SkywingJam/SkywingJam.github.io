01 / THE HOMELAB

One small machine, three layers.

A Mac mini on a shelf at home. Its job is to keep
a second copy of things and stay out of the way.

I.

Hardware

Everything sits on one Mac mini and one external drive. There’s no RAID, and nothing here is the only copy of anything important, so a dead drive means downtime, not loss. That trade is deliberate.

Mac mini M4
16 GB unified memory, 256 GB internal SSD. Idles around 5–6 W, by my own reading rather than a proper measurement.
ALWAYS ON
Samsung T7, 2 TB
Photo library, sync folders and their versions, the Time Machine image, manual iPhone backups, the audio library.
SINGLE DISK
II.

Services

Each runs as its own process, and only one lives in a container.

Immich
A second copy of photos and videos, viewable from any device.
COMPOSE / ORBSTACK
AdGuard Home
DNS filtering at home and for devices on Tailscale.
NATIVE / LAUNCHD
Syncthing
Two-way sync between devices. Staggered versions: about 30 days for personal files, about 180 for work and uni.
NATIVE
SMB share
Time Machine target for the MacBook (about 800 GB quota), and a direct way to fetch files when a sync client misbehaves.
MACOS SHARING
NeoKikoeru
A self-hosted audio library, behind an nginx proxy that carries my extensions (FIG. 002).
NATIVE / LAUNCHD
AU Job Board
My job-search tool. Collects, filters and ranks listings every morning; the web view stays up.
LAUNCHD / 07:30

HOME WI-FI Tailscale is off. DNS goes to AdGuard on the Mac mini over the home network; everything else goes straight out through the router.

AWAY On mobile data or someone else’s Wi-Fi, Tailscale connects by itself. DNS still travels home to AdGuard through the tunnel; everything else leaves from the network I’m on.

PUBLIC WI-FI When I choose to, on hotel or public networks, the Mac mini becomes an exit node: all traffic goes home through the tunnel first and leaves from there.

III.

Network

Getting back to home from anywhere, without routing everything through home.

Split tunnel
Tailscale switches off by network name on home Wi-Fi and connects by itself anywhere else. Only DNS is sent home.
VPN ON DEMAND
Subnet router
Remote devices reach services at their usual home addresses.
TAILSCALE
Exit node
Off by default. I turn it on by hand on hotel or public Wi-Fi.
ON REQUEST
DNS fallback
None, on purpose. If home DNS fails I want to notice straight away; the fix is a manual switch.
DELIBERATE

WHAT IT ISN’T Not a cluster, no SLA, no monitoring dashboard: I read logs when something feels off. The limits I chose, and the one I haven’t proven yet, are under trade-offs.

002 / EXTENSION LAYER In use

A layer on top, host untouched.

NeoKikoeru is a closed-source audio library I host at home. Rather than edit its files, an nginx proxy injects my own JavaScript into the pages it serves. Five modules live there: a waveform heatmap, live subtitles with picture-in-picture, watch later, work marks with filters, and a parametric EQ on Web Audio that imports Equalizer APO presets.

The hard parts belong to the host: when its player sets a source and starts playing, what iOS Safari lets an AudioContext do, and sharing state between scripts that load separately. The host stays pinned at 0.26.3. Moving to 3.x means a reindex and retesting the whole injection layer, and that hasn’t been worth it yet.

03 / FIELD NOTES

Back in sight.

The times something in the background surfaced: what broke, what I looked at, and what I can and can’t claim afterwards.

OCT 2026
01 / RECOVERY

Leaving OneDrive without losing a file.

OneDrive’s File Provider had to go, on two accounts. The university one was simple: most files were already local, so I checked them against the copy on the Mac mini and unlinked. The personal one wasn’t. Some files showed as not downloaded, but a script found they could still be read from the cache. I saved the cache first, reset OneDrive, and restored what the reset dropped from that saved copy. rsync --ignore-existing added 43,583 files back without overwriting anything.

02 / BACKUP

Time Machine that wouldn’t mount.

The SMB share was reachable, but the backup image on it wasn’t. backupd logged an invalid mountpoint and a disconnected disk image (error 70), and a manual tmutil startbackup reproduced it. After a restart it got as far as mounting the image, and the backup completed.

LIMITS The root cause is still unknown, and a backup that finishes is not a restore I’ve tested.

10 AUG 2026
03 / NETWORK

Dropouts that kept a schedule.

The first time the connection dropped I checked my own DNS; it was fine, and the ISP had been doing maintenance, so I waited. The second time it looked periodic, so I read the router log. The bottom was a wall of Failed to get IP for interface ppp0. Comparing that with the healthy entries at the top led to the middle: No response to 9 echo-requests, then Serial link appears to be disconnected. Superloop confirmed the service had moved from PPPoE to IPoE. After switching the router over, the dropouts stopped.

LIMITS The log also showed Wi-Fi chip errors that I never tied to the problem. Why the old session failed on their side, I can’t say.

UNDATED
04 / OPERATIONS

Docker Desktop to OrbStack.

On a 16 GB machine, Docker Desktop was holding 8–10 GB or more over long runs, with the odd stall and growing swap. Nothing was down; I wanted the memory back. I looked at Apple’s container tool first, but Compose support, Immich’s upgrade path, volume permissions and data safety pointed to OrbStack at the time. It took over the existing containers and Immich kept running. Later I saw it using about 1.77 GB.

LIMITS Two observations under different conditions: not a benchmark, and not proof of a leak.

04 / TRADE-OFFS

How it fails, on purpose.

Some limits are choices; one is still homework.
Knowing which is which is most of running this.

I.

One disk, no RAID.

Everything lives on one Mac mini and one external drive. If the drive dies, services stop until it’s replaced, but nothing is lost, because nothing here is the only copy. DNS follows the same rule: no automatic fallback, so a failure shows up straight away instead of hiding behind a backup resolver.

KEPT ON PURPOSE
II.

The host stays on 0.26.3.

Moving NeoKikoeru to 3.x means a full reindex and retesting every injected script against a new player. So far that cost has outweighed the upgrade, so the old version stays, and I keep the trade in view rather than pretending it’s free.

REVISIT WHEN IT PAYS
III.

A restore I’ve actually run.

Backups finish and their history is browsable, but I haven’t restored a file through Time Machine or Syncthing, rebuilt the machine, or rehearsed recovery after a power cut. Immich’s database has no scheduled backup that I know of, and I haven’t walked every network path in one by one. Until one of those works end to end, recovery is a claim, not a result.
